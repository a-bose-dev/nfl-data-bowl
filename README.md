# Who Gets Open? Separation Above Expected vs Yards per Route Run (NFL 2021, Weeks 1-8)

The NFL is a game of inches, and nothing demonstrates that more than the separation a receiver gets. The great route runners of the league like Cooper Kupp and Davante Adams have one thing in mind - how can I make as much space between me and the defender tasked with covering me?

So we built a tool where we mapped separation above expected (SOE): how much more or less space a receiver creates at pass release than a typical receiver would against the same coverage and from the same alignment, so players are judged against the situation they actually faced. Using NFL Next Gen Stats tracking data and PFF coverage and scouting data from weeks 1 to 8 of the 2021 season, we measured the distance from every route runner to the nearest defender, compared it with the average for that situation, and plotted each receiver's SOE against his yards per route run to show who is best at creating space and who is most productive with the space they get. The four resulting groups are receivers who create space and produce, create space but lack the yardage, produce without space, or do neither, and dropdown filters for coverage type (man, zone, red zone), alignment (slot or wide) and formation let users explore any situation.

Coaches can use it to identify receivers who could improve their separation or how productive they are with the space they have, fans and broadcasters can use it to enrich how they watch and explain a game, and scouts can use it to help spot the next great route runner.
