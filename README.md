# Who Gets Open? Receiver Separation vs Yards per Route Run (NFL 2021, Weeks 1-8)

The NFL is a game of inches, and nothing demonstrates that more than the separation a receiver gets. The great route runners of the league like Cooper Kupp and Davante Adams have one thing in mind - how can I make as much space between me and the defender tasked with covering me? So, we measured receiver separation (the distance to the nearest defender at pass release) for every route runner in weeks 1 to 8 of the 2021 season, using NFL Next Gen Stats tracking data and PFF coverage and scouting data. We then plotted each receiver's average separation against yards per route run, splitting receivers into four groups: those who get open and produce, those who get open but lack the yardage, those who produce without separation, and those who do neither. The visualisation has dropdown filters for coverage type (man, zone, red zone), alignment (slot or wide) and offensive formation, because how open a receiver gets depends heavily on the situation he is in. Separation uses the nearest defender as a proxy for the covering defender, and results for individual players rest on a limited number of routes. Limitations with this include the small sample set (only games from W1 - W8 in 2021 were available), and nearest defender is not an actual representation of who is covering them, only an estimation. All code and outputs are in this repository.
